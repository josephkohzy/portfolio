Onboarding Flow Unify — Workplace Communication Platform

UX Writing Notes

Welcome screens should welcome, not instruct.

Most onboarding screens fail because they celebrate the product before the user has experienced any value. This welcome copy flips the frame — "Your team is already here. You just haven't arrived yet" — so the user feels like they're catching up to something real, not starting from zero. It reduces the psychological distance between signup and first value, without a single feature name.

Presuppose the good outcome "Your team is already here" implies connection already exists — users just need to arrive.
Name the action, not the feature "Connect Slack workspace" beats "Add integration" — it describes what happens, not what it is.
Earn the secondary CTA "Or start with email instead" is permission, not a fallback — it respects user workflow without friction.
Empty State MYIO — List Maker App

Copy Intent

Empty states shouldn't just describe emptiness — they should make starting feel irresistible. The specific example ("your top 5 albums right now") lowers the blank-page anxiety that stops first-time engagement.

Push Notifications Unify & MYIO

Copy Intent

Each notification earns its push. The copy names who did what, quotes enough to make opening feel worth it, and treats "nothing new" as a positive — not a non-event. The "all caught up" notification acknowledges the user's time, which builds trust in the channel over time.

Tooltips & Helper Text Unify — Settings

Copy Intent

Good tooltip copy doesn't repeat the label — it answers the unspoken question: "But what actually happens when I turn this on?" The focus mode tooltip specifies the edge case (urgent items still get through) so users can trust the setting without anxiety.

Subtext does the heavy lifting ".CSV · compatible with Notion & Sheets" tells users what they can do with their data before they even ask.

Game Pitch / Pull Quote Touché — Romcom Visual Novel

"Every route ends differently. Every choice you skip is a version of them you'll never see."

Written as the pitch hook for Touché's branching narrative structure. Visual novel marketing lives or dies on making the player feel like their decisions matter before they've made a single one. This line sells replayability without using the word — and puts the emotional cost of inaction front and centre, which is exactly the genre's appeal.

Long-form / Game Description Touché — Romcom Visual Novel

Steam page — game description

Amélie didn't plan on staying. One semester abroad, a few cultural misunderstandings, and a flight home at the end of it. Clean. Simple. Predictable.

Then she met him in the stairwell of the wrong building at the wrong time, and the semester got complicated.

Touché is a romcom visual novel about two people who are terrible at saying what they mean. Multiple routes. Branching dialogue. An ending that depends entirely on how honest you're willing to be.

Some things don't translate. Feelings aren't one of them.
